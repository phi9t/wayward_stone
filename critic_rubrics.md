# Critic Skill Evaluation Rubrics
## Wayward Stone: Day Three — Prose Assessment Framework

---

## Overview

This document provides structured evaluation rubrics for assessing prose quality in the Wayward Stone manuscript. It synthesizes the original 10-point criteria from `writing_style.md` with macro-level feedback from draft reviews and line-level pattern analysis.

**Assessment Philosophy:** Each rubric evaluates prose independently. A chapter can score high on one criterion while scoring low on another. Overall quality emerges from pattern across all rubrics.

---

## Rubric 1: Exposition Density & "Telling"

**Definition:** The degree to which the narrative explains themes, stakes, or character states explicitly rather than dramatizing them through action, dialogue, and sensory detail.

| Score | Descriptor | Indicators |
|-------|-----------|------------|
| **1-2** | **Critical Warning** | Characters explain the plot to each other; scene endings summarize meaning; narrator interprets emotions |
| **3-4** | **Needs Work** | Occasional meta-commentary; some dialogue functions as character-sheet exposition; occasional interpretive closures |
| **5-6** | **Acceptable** | Most information dramatized; occasional explanatory passages; mixed but functional |
| **7-8** | **Strong** | Information embedded in conflict; exposition paid for with tension; minimal summary |
| **9-10** | **Excellent** | Everything dramatized; reader infers rather than receives; exposition is invisible |

**Red Flag Indicators:**
- Dialogue like: "You keep doing this. Learning impossible things..."
- Narrator like: "I realized... [interpretive closure]"
- Scene endings that state meaning rather than showing action/image/decision

**Revision Tactic:**
> Replace explanatory dialogue with tactical questions or physical beats. End scenes on images, decisions, or implications rather than interpretations.

---

## Rubric 2: Stakes, Secrecy & Conflict Resolution

**Definition:** The clarity, cost, and uncertainty surrounding conflicts. High-stakes narratives feature unclear outcomes, meaningful costs, and protagonists who must navigate obstacles rather than receiving easy answers.

| Score | Descriptor | Indicators |
|-------|-----------|------------|
| **1-2** | **Critical Warning** | Threats stated explicitly; conflicts resolve transparently; antagonists explain motivations |
| **3-4** | **Needs Work** | Some friction but clean resolutions; antagonists partially telegraphed; secrets revealed without cost |
| **5-6** | **Acceptable** | Mixed resolution patterns; some conflicts paid for; occasional opacity |
| **7-8** | **Strong** | Conflicts resolved with trade-offs; secrets protected; antagonists implied rather than stated |
| **9-10** | **Excellent** | Every revelation changes the board; consequences follow actions; uncertainty maintained |

**Red Flag Indicators:**
- "Direct confession → quick acceptance" patterns (e.g., Arwyl evaluation)
- Antagonists delivering explicit threat monologues
- Secrets revealed through speech rather than discovered through action

**Revision Tactic:**
> Add resistance to reveals. Ensure every answer produces a new problem. Make antagonists' power visible through consequences, not statements.

---

## Rubric 3: Character Agency & Emotional Realism

**Definition:** The degree to which characters make choices that drive narrative momentum, particularly in high-stakes moments. Emotional expressions should emerge from behavior rather than summary.

| Score | Descriptor | Indicators |
|-------|-----------|------------|
| **1-2** | **Critical Warning** | Characters react rather than act; emotions stated explicitly; agency ceded to plot |
| **3-4** | **Needs Work** | Some proactive choices; emotions partially grounded; occasional passivity |
| **5-6** | **Acceptable** | Mixed patterns; characters act but with prompting; emotions shown with some telling |
| **7-8** | **Strong** | Characters drive plot through choices; emotions physical/behavioral; agency maintained |
| **9-10** | **Excellent** | Every character moment advances story; emotions emerge through action; agency central |

**Red Flag Indicators:**
- Denna delivering warnings rather than acting strategically
- Kvothe receiving information rather than pursuing it
- Emotions described: "She felt afraid" rather than: "Her hands shook"

**Revision Tactic:**
> Hide exposition in character desire and tactics. Reveal fear through tremor, bruise, avoidance. Give characters goals that complicate others' goals.

---

## Rubric 4: Voice Originality vs. Derivative Patterns

**Definition:** The balance between stylistic imitation of source material and distinctive prose execution. Strong voice maintains Rothfuss-like cadence without relying on repeated tics or crutches.

| Score | Descriptor | Indicators |
|-------|-----------|------------|
| **1-2** | **Critical Warning** | Heavy reliance on specific tics; "particular" used as filler; filter verbs dominate |
| **3-4** | **Needs Work** | Occasional crutches; some variety but repeated patterns; some originality emerging |
| **5-6** | **Acceptable** | Balanced imitation and originality; tics present but not overwhelming |
| **7-8** | **Strong** | Mostly original execution; cadences earned rather than borrowed; distinctive voice |
| **9-10** | **Excellent** | Voice indistinguishable from source quality; zero tics; wholly distinctive |

**Pattern Count Targets (per chapter):**
| Pattern | Warning Threshold | Target |
|---------|------------------|--------|
| "particular" | >5 occurrences | 0-1 |
| Vague "something" | >3 occurrences | 0-1 |
| Filter verbs ("I saw/I heard/I felt") | >3 occurrences | 0-1 |
| Repeated tricolons | >2 per page | 0-1 |
| Explained metaphors | >2 per chapter | 0 |

**Revision Tactic:**
> Cut 80-90% of crutch words. Replace "particular X" with specific qualifier or delete. Replace "I could see" with direct perception.

## Rubric 5: Dialogue as Character

**Definition:** The degree to which speakers are identifiable without attribution tags, and dialogue functions as characterization rather than information transfer.

| Score | Descriptor | Indicators |
|-------|-----------|------------|
| **1-2** | **Critical Warning** | Speakers indistinguishable; dialogue functions as exposition; tags required for clarity |
| **3-4** | **Needs Work** | Most speakers recognizable; occasional indistinguishability; some information-dump dialogue |
| **5-6** | **Acceptable** | Mixed patterns; tags sometimes helpful; dialogue advances plot and character |
| **7-8** | **Strong** | Speakers clearly distinguishable; dialogue characterizes; minimal tags needed |
| **9-10** | **Excellent** | Voice distinctive; dialogue functions as subtext; attribution unnecessary |

**Voice Fingerprints:**
| Character | Markers |
|-----------|----------|
| **Kvothe (told)** | Articulate, sometimes grandiose; academic vocabulary; self-aware wit |
| **Kote (frame)** | Measured, neutral; warmth in unguarded moments |
| **Bast** | Mercurial; "Reshi"; affection/threat in same breath |
| **Chronicler** | Precise; questioning; practical |
| **Simmon** | Earnest; warm; pragmatic concerns |
| **Wilem** | Economical; dry; deadpan observations |
| **Denna** | Witty deflection; guardedness; courage beneath vulnerability |

**Revision Tactic:**
> Cover dialogue tags. If speakers indistinguishable, rewrite for voice fingerprint. Eliminate information-dump dialogue; embed exposition in conflict.

## Rubric 6: Sensory Grounding & Physicality

**Definition:** The degree to which abstract concepts (magic, emotion, setting) are anchored in concrete sensory experience.

| Score | Descriptor | Indicators |
|-------|-----------|------------|
| **1-2** | **Critical Warning** | Abstract concepts stated without physical correlate; magic without sensation |
| **3-4** | **Needs Work** | Some grounding; occasional abstract passages; mixed execution |
| **5-6** | **Acceptable** | Most concepts grounded; magic has physical texture; setting visible |
| **7-8** | **Strong** | Consistent grounding; sensory detail rich but not ornamental; body-based magic |
| **9-10** | **Excellent** | Every abstract concept physicalized; reader feels cold/taste/sound; magic embodied |

**Red Flag Indicators:**
- "He felt the power of Naming flow through him" (ungrounded)
- "She was afraid" (stated emotion)
- Setting described in abstraction rather than image

**Revision Tactic:**
> Ground every abstraction. Magic = temperature change, taste, physical strain. Emotion = hands, posture, breath. Setting = specific objects, textures, smells.

## Rubric 7: Pacing Through Detail

**Definition:** The distribution of narrative attention: expanded treatment for important moments, compressed treatment for transitions.

| Score | Descriptor | Indicators |
|-------|-----------|------------|
| **1-2** | **Critical Warning** | Wrong moments expanded; climaxes compressed; pacing inconsistent |
| **3-4** | **Needs Work** | Some pacing awareness; occasional imbalance; partial execution |
| **5-6** | **Acceptable** | General awareness of importance; balanced but imperfect; readable rhythm |
| **7-8** | **Strong** | Clear differentiation; action accelerated; revelations decelerated; breathing prose |
| **9-10** | **Excellent** | Perfect rhythm; every moment gets appropriate treatment; time dilates appropriately |

**Application Rules:**
- **Discovery/Revelation:** Slow. Every detail of room, stone, artifact. Reader should feel time dilating.
- **Transition/Travel:** Compressed. "A sentence, maybe two." Don't narrate walks unless walk matters.
- **Action:** Accelerate. Short sentences. Momentum.
- **Confrontation:** Decelerate. Granular. Each beat clear.

## Rubric 8: Thematic Motif Consistency

**Definition:** The deployment and accumulation of recurring images, themes, and symbols across the narrative.

| Score | Descriptor | Indicators |
|-------|-----------|------------|
| **1-2** | **Critical Warning** | Motifs absent or disconnected; repetition without accumulation |
| **3-4** | **Needs Work** | Some motifs present; inconsistent deployment; partial accumulation |
| **5-6** | **Acceptable** | Core motifs present; some accumulation; acceptable variation |
| **7-8** | **Strong** | Consistent motif deployment; clear trajectory; accumulation visible |
| **9-10** | **Excellent** | Motifs form thematic architecture; each repetition adds weight; trajectory clear |

**Primary Motifs (Wayward Stone):**
| Motif | First Appearance | Accumulation Pattern |
|-------|-----------------|---------------------|
| **Silence** | Frame opening | Atmospheric → Ominous → Revelatory |
| **Doors** | Plot central | Literal → Metaphorical → Thematic |
| **Names** | Naming magic | Functional → Symbolic → Identity |
| **Fire** | Chandrian signs | Threat → Metaphor → Inner fire |
| **Music** | Kvothe's identity | Joy → Grief → Absence → Return |
| **Locks/Keys** | Thrice-locked chest | Literal → Symbol → Character |

**Revision Tactic:**
> Track motif occurrences. Ensure each deployment adds to trajectory. Cut repetitions that don't advance accumulation.

---

## Rubric 9: Unreliable Narrator Awareness

**Definition:** The degree to which Kvothe's telling reveals gaps between the teller and the tale, demonstrating consciousness of narrative construction.

| Score | Descriptor | Indicators |
|-------|-----------|------------|
| **1-2** | **Critical Warning** | Narrator claims objectivity; no gaps acknowledged; omissions invisible |
| **3-4** | **Needs Work** | Occasional self-awareness; partial acknowledgment; some gaps visible |
| **5-6** | **Acceptable** | Periodic awareness; some justification patterns; reader can sense gap |
| **7-8** | **Strong** | Clear self-consciousness; gaps acknowledged; ego/pain evident |
| **9-10** | **Excellent** | Sophisticated unreliability; reader senses gap throughout; tale ≠ teller |

**Reliability Markers:**
- **Over-explanation:** Justifying actions not proud of
- **Omission:** Skipping vulnerable moments
- **Self-deprecation:** Performing pride as humility (or vice versa)
- **Chronicler/Bast contradiction:** Other characters highlighting narrator gaps

**Revision Tactic:**
> Let contradictions emerge through other characters or physical details. Include moments of vulnerability or uncertainty.

---

## Rubric 10: Ending Impact

**Definition:** The resonance and memorability of chapter/section endings. Strong endings land with emotional reverberation rather than trailing off.

| Score | Descriptor | Indicators |
|-------|-----------|------------|
| **1-2** | **Critical Warning** | Endings summarize; conclusions trail off; no lasting impression |
| **3-4** | **Needs Work** | Occasional strong endings; mixed quality; some resonance |
| **5-6** | **Acceptable** | Consistent quality; readable conclusions; adequate closure |
| **7-8** | **Strong** | Memorable closing chords; image-based endings; emotional reverberation |
| **9-10** | **Excellent** | Perfect closing chords; quotable; reader pauses before continuing |

**Ending Patterns:**
| Strong | Weak |
|---------|------|
| Image (visual, specific) | "And then they went to sleep..." |
| Decision (character commits) | Summary sentence |
| Implication (open question) | Interpretation stated |
| Action (physical beat) | Trailing off |

## Quick Assessment Checklist

For rapid chapter evaluation, apply this 10-question check:

| # | Question | Pass/Fail |
|---|----------|------------|
| 1 | Did characters explain plot or live it? | ___ |
| 2 | Did conflicts resolve with cost? | ___ |
| 3 | Did characters act or react? | ___ |
| 4 | Were "particular"/filter verbs present? | ___ |
| 5 | Could you identify speakers without tags? | ___ |
| 6 | Was magic/emotion physically grounded? | ___ |
| 7 | Did pacing match importance? | ___ |
| 8 | Did motifs accumulate? | ___ |
| 9 | Did narrator show awareness of unreliability? | ___ |
| 10 | Did the ending land with impact? | ___ |

**Scoring:** 8-10 passes = strong chapter; 5-7 passes = needs revision; <5 passes = critical rework needed

## Application Workflow

1. **Read chapter** for holistic impression
2. **Apply rubric scores** (1-10 each)
3. **Count patterns** using reference table
4. **Identify lowest scores** for priority revision
5. **Target specific issues:**
   - Score <5 on any rubric = structural rework
   - Pattern count high = mechanical cleanup pass
6. **Revise and re-score**
